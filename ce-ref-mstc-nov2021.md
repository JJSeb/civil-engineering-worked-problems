## Questions 1–10

### 1. Inverse-square light intensity
For inverse-square variation, \(I=k/x^2\). The scan gives $I=80$ foot-candles at $x=2$ ft, hence $k=80(2^2)=320$. At $I=5$, $x=\sqrt{320/5}=8$ ft. **Answer: 8 ft (C).**

### 2. Endangered-species estimate
The printed model is \(y=\dfrac{1883}{1+7.892(1.097^{-t})}\), with \(t\) years after 1980. For 2020, \(t=40\), giving \(y\approx1576\). The table gives 1137 for 2007, so \(1576-1137=439\). **Answer: 439 (A).** The source solution prints 1337 in its subtraction line, a typo; its stated result uses 1137.

### 3. Roofers working together
Their combined rate is \(1/26+1/39=5/78\) house/h. Thus \(t=1/(5/78)=15.6\) h. **Answer: 15.6 h (C).**

### 4. Falling object from the Millau Viaduct
The scan gives \(h(t)=-16t^2+1125\) ft. At \(t=6\) s, \(h=1125-16(36)=549\) ft. **Answer: 549 ft (A).**

### 5. Insurance commission — key conflict
The stated rates give \(0.01(\mathrm{Php}\,200{,}000)+0.02(\mathrm{Php}\,100{,}000)=\mathrm{Php}\,4{,}000\). **Computed answer: Php4,000 (A).** The printed key says **B (Php5,000)**, which does not follow from the stated policy amounts and commission rates; the source's worked calculation also gives Php4,000.

### 6. Rational solutions
For \(f(x)=3x^4+14x^3+14x^2-8x-8\), substitution verifies \(f(-2)=0\) and \(f(-2/3)=0\), the rational pair listed in choice D. **Answer: D.**

### 7. Gompertz model
The scan gives \(L=1000\), with 5 hospitals switched in year 1 and 70 more in year 2, so cumulative \(D_1=5\) at \(t=1\) and \(D_2=75\) at \(t=2\). For \(D=Le^{-ce^{-dt}}\), take logs twice: \(A=\ln[\ln(L/D)]=\ln c-dt\). Then \(A_1=1.66739\), \(A_2=0.95176\), \(d=A_1-A_2=0.71563\), and \(\ln c=A_1+d=2.3830\). Thus \(c=10.8376\). **Answer: C.**

### 8. Logistic parameter
For \(y=k/(1+be^{-cx})\), put \(x=0\), \(y=3\), and \(k=105\): \(3=105/(1+b)\), so \(b=34\). **Answer: 34 (D).**

### 9. Matching lighthouse flashes
Over the 12-second common cycle, compare the colors over each 1-second interval. They match during 0–1 s (red), 3–4 s (white), 7–8 s (white), and 11–12 s (white): 4 s total. Thus the fraction is \(4/12=1/3\). **Computed answer: B (1/3).** The printed key and worked solution say A (1/6), but omit three white-on-white intervals.

### 10. Freeway distance on a grid
The endpoint differences are \(115-25=90\) streets and \(64-8=56\) avenues: \(\sqrt{90^2+56^2}=106\) blocks. At 850 ft/block, \(106(850)/5280=17.06\) mi. **Answer: 17 mi (A).**

## Questions 11–20

### 11. Triangle angles
Let the smallest angle be \(x\). The angles are \(x\), \(x+10^\circ\), and \(x+80^\circ\). Thus \(3x+90^\circ=180^\circ\), so the angles are \(30^\circ,40^\circ,110^\circ\). **Answer: D.**

### 12. Bridge length by triangulation
The 280 m perpendicular offset is opposite the bridge length, and the angle at C is \(49^\circ50'\). Thus \(\tan(49^\circ50')=AB/280\), so \(AB=280\tan(49^\circ50')=331.73\) m. **Answer: A.**

### 13. Refraction at a water surface
Snell's law gives \(n_a\sin\theta_i=n_w\sin\theta_r\). With \(n_a=1.00\), \(n_w=1.33\), and \(\theta_i=24.5^\circ\), \(\theta_r=\sin^{-1}(\sin24.5^\circ/1.33)=18.2^\circ\). **Answer: D.**

### 14. Tower height
Let the horizontal distance from the nearer point B to the tower be \(x\), and tower height \(h\). Then \(h=x\tan21.8^\circ=(x+50)\tan19.9^\circ\); solving gives \(h\approx191\) ft. **Answer: C.**

### 15. Circle inscribed in a sector
Sector area is \(96\pi\,\mathrm{m^2}\), and \(96\pi=\tfrac12(24)^2\theta\), so \(\theta=\pi/3=60^\circ\). The small circle's center lies on the angle bisector; tangency gives \(\sin30^\circ=r/(24-r)\), hence \(r=8\) m. **Answer: D.**

### 16. Flagstaff height
Let the square side be \(s=\sqrt{160{,}000}=400\) ft and place consecutive corners at \(A=(0,0),B=(400,0),C=(400,400)\). The equal \(60^\circ\) angles at B and C imply the observer O is equidistant from B and C, so its y-coordinate is 200 ft. If O=(x,200), the \(45^\circ\) view at A gives \(h^2=x^2+200^2\); the \(60^\circ\) view at B gives \(h^2=3[(x-400)^2+200^2]\). Solving yields the physically relevant \(x\approx317.16\) ft and \(h\approx375\) ft. Convert: \(375(0.3048)=114.3\) m. **Answer: B (114.32 m).**

### 17. Triangle side lengths
Let the two equal sides be \(x\) and the third be \(x+9\). From \(2x+(x+9)=93\), \(x=28\) cm, so the longest side is \(37\) cm. **Answer: A.**

### 18. Zooming photograph
Both dimensions increase at \(2\,\mathrm{km/s}\), so \(A(t)=(5+2t)(7+2t)\) and the original area is \(35\,\mathrm{km^2}\). Set \((5+2t)(7+2t)=175\); the positive root is \(t=3.63\) s. **Answer: C.**

### 19. Map scale
The map distance 15 mm is 1.5 cm, representing \(1.5(40)=60\) m on the ground. With a 1.0 m contour interval, \(S=(1/60)(100\%)=1.667\%\). **Answer: C.**

### 20. Washer area
Area \(=\pi R^2-\pi r^2=\pi(R-r)(R+r)\). **Answer: B.**

## Questions 21–30

### 21. Pyramid cut into equal weights
For similar pyramids, volume is proportional to the cube of the height. The top pyramid must have half the original volume, so its height is \(15/\sqrt[3]{2}\). The cut's distance above the base is \(15-15/\sqrt[3]{2}=3.10\) m. **Answer: B.**

### 22. Moon surface area
For a sphere of radius \(1080\) mi, \(A=4\pi r^2=4\pi(1080)^2\approx14{,}657{,}415\) mi². **Answer: B.**

### 23. Perpendicular path to the ball's trajectory
The ball's line has slope \(4/3\), so a perpendicular path has slope \(-3/4\). Through \(P=(35,-20)\), its equation is \(y+20=-\tfrac34(x-35)\). **Answer: B.**

### 24. Paraboloid focus
For a parabolic cross-section, \(x^2=4fy\). The rim radius is 6 m at depth 2 m, so \(36=4f(2)\), giving focal distance \(f=4.5\) m. **Answer: B.**

### 25. Vector magnitude and direction
For vector \((-5,-5)\), \(r=\sqrt{(-5)^2+(-5)^2}=5\sqrt2\). It lies in quadrant III, so \(\theta=225^\circ=5\pi/4\). **Answer: C.**

### 26. Minimum-area open box
Let width be \(w\), length \(2w\), and height \(h\). Volume gives \(2w^2h=1.78\), and open-top area is \(S=2w^2+6wh=2w^2+5.34/w\). Setting \(dS/dw=4w-5.34/w^2=0\) gives \(w=1.101\) m. **Answer: A (1.10 m).**

### 27. Maximum trough volume
For top width \(b\), the isosceles triangle height is \(\sqrt{5^2-(b/2)^2}\); its area is \(A=\tfrac12b\sqrt{25-b^2/4}\). Maximizing area (and thus fixed-length trough volume) gives \(b=5\sqrt2=7.07\) ft. **Answer: D (7.1 ft).**

### 28. Falling water level in cone
Similar triangles give \(r/h=4/10=0.4\), so \(V=\tfrac13\pi(0.4h)^2h\). Differentiate: \(dV/dt=0.16\pi h^2(dh/dt)\). At \(h=5\) m and \(dV/dt=-15\,\mathrm{m^3/min}\), \(dh/dt=-15/[0.16\pi(25)]=-1.19\,\mathrm{m/min}\). **Answer: C.**

### 29. Instantaneous velocity
From \(s=-4.9t^2+119.2\), \(v=ds/dt=-9.8t\); at 3 s, \(v=-29.4\) m/s. **Answer: B.**

### 30. Power climbing a hill
At steady speed, the engine must overcome the component of weight down the slope and the stated 750 N resistance:

\[
F=mg\sin10^\circ+750=1500(9.81)\sin10^\circ+750=3303\,\mathrm N.
\]

The speed is \(80/3.6=22.22\,\mathrm{m/s}\), so \(P=Fv=73.4\,\mathrm{kW}\). Converting with \(1\,\mathrm{hp}=0.746\,\mathrm{kW}\) gives \(98.4\,\mathrm{hp}\). **Answer: D (98.5 hp).**

## Questions 31–40

### 31. Loss of gravitational potential energy
The box drops vertically by \(5\sin30^\circ=2.5\) m. The potential-energy change is \(\Delta PE=mg(h_f-h_i)=-5(9.81)(2.5)=-122.6\,\mathrm J\). **Answer: D (−123 J).** Friction and initial speed do not affect this gravitational potential-energy change.

### 32. Isothermal air-compression power
For ideal isothermal compression, \(P=p_1\dot V\ln(p_2/p_1)\). Use inlet absolute pressure \(p_1=0.101\,\mathrm{MPa}\), outlet absolute pressure \(p_2=8.01\,\mathrm{bar}=0.801\,\mathrm{MPa}\), and inlet flow \(\dot V=2.8/60=0.04667\,\mathrm{m^3/s}\):

\[
P=(0.101\times10^6)(0.04667)\ln(0.801/0.101)=9.75\,\mathrm{kW}.
\]

**Answer: A (9.7 kW).**

### 33. Monthly pump power and maintenance cost
The electrical input is \(200(0.746)/0.85=175.53\,\mathrm{kW}\). Annual energy cost is \(175.53(400)(\mathrm{Php}\,4)=\mathrm{Php}\,280{,}848\). Add annual maintenance of Php5,000 and divide by 12:

\[
C_m=(280{,}848+5{,}000)/12=\mathrm{Php}\,23{,}820.67\text{/month}.
\]

Rounded to the printed value, **Answer: D (Php23,820.50).** The prompt asks for monthly operating power and maintenance cost; it does not ask to include capital recovery.

### 34. Refinery capacity-cost estimate
The capacity exponent table gives \(n=0.57\) for a small refinery. Scale total cost using \(C_1=C_2(Q_1/Q_2)^n\), with \(C_2=Php190\) million at \(Q_2=2\) million barrels/year. Then divide each estimated total cost by its capacity:

\[
\frac{C}{Q}=\mathrm{Php}\,95(2/Q)^{0.43}.
\]

This gives approximately Php128, Php95, and Php80 per barrel/year for capacities 1M, 2M, and 3M. **Answer: C.**

### 35. At least one defective in a sample
Use the complement: all five are good. Sampling without replacement gives

\[
P(\ge1\text{ defective})=1-\frac{\binom{95}{5}}{\binom{100}{5}}=0.231\text{ (approximately)}.
\]

**Answer: A (0.23).**

### 36. Flood probability — key conflict
Standardize the 57-inch threshold: \(z=(57-50)/4=1.75\). The upper tail is \(P(Z>1.75)\approx0.0401\). **Calculated probability: 0.0401.** No option matches; the printed key indicates D (0.401), ten times the normal-tail result.

### 37. Relay reliability
The statement says the message passes if all four relays are closed. With independent relay probabilities \(0.5,0.7,0.8,0.8\),

\[
P=0.5(0.7)(0.8)(0.8)=0.224.
\]

**Answer: A (0.224).**

### 38. Tennis-set sequence
For Father–Champion–Father, the chance of at least two consecutive wins is \(0.8(0.4)+0.4(0.8)-0.8(0.4)(0.8)=0.384\). For Champion–Father–Champion it is \(0.4(0.8)+0.8(0.4)-0.4(0.8)(0.4)=0.512\). Choose the second sequence. **Answer: D.** The scan duplicates one option label, but D states Champion–Father–Champion with probability 0.512.

### 39. Traverse azimuth
The adjusted triangle angles are A=52°25′, B=53°04′, C=74°31′, and azimuth AB=54°10′. Reverse to get BA=234°10′. Taking the triangle interior at B gives BC=234°10′−53°04′=181°06′; thus CB=1°06′. At C, the direction CA is \(1^\circ06'−74^\circ31'=−73^\circ25'\), or \(286^\circ35'\) modulo 360°. **Answer: B (286°35′).**

### 40. Wave height from steepness
For a deep-water wave, the source uses \(\lambda=gT^2/(2\pi)\). With \(T=7.47\) s, \(\lambda=87.123\) m and \(H=S\lambda=0.14(87.123)=12.20\) m. **Answer by the source method: C.** **Caution:** the question also gives depth 9 m, which is not deep water for this wavelength (deep-water criterion is approximately \(d\ge\lambda/2\)). Using the finite-depth dispersion equation instead gives about \(H=8.75\) m, not among the choices; the keyed choice relies on the deep-water approximation despite the stated depth.

## Questions 41–50

### 41. Typhoon wave velocity — source approximation
The printed solution treats a deep-water wave at the boundary \(d=\lambda/2\), so \(\lambda=2(150)=300\) m. Deep-water celerity is \(v=\sqrt{g\lambda/(2\pi)}=\sqrt{g(150)/\pi}=21.64\) m/s, or \(77.9\) km/h. **Answer: C.** This is an approximation based on the deep-water threshold; the question itself does not provide a period or wavelength.

### 42. Basic and standard time
Basic time is observed time times performance rating: \(7(0.90)=6.30\) min. With an 8% allowance on standard time, \(T_s=6.30/(1-0.08)=6.85\) min. **Answer: B (6.30 min; 6.85 min).**

### 43. Cleaning crew standard time
Average observed time for “Empty bins” is \((1.00+0.90+0.75+1.20+1.40+0.60+0.80)/7=0.95\) min; normal time is \(0.95(0.90)=0.855\) min. Average for “Vacuum Floor” is \((3.00+4.50+3.50+3.00+3.75+4.25+4.25)/7=3.75\) min; normal time is \(3.75(1.25)=4.6875\) min. Total normal time is 5.5425 min. With 15% allowance, standard time is \(5.5425/(1-0.15)=6.521\) min. **Answer: D.**

### 44. Wheel-loader production
Basic cycle time for average material is 0.50 min. From the low-resolution travel chart, 61 m one-way distance at 6% grade gives about 0.30 min for haul and return, so the cycle is about 0.80 min. Effective cycles per hour are \(50/0.80=62.5\), and production is \(2.68(62.5)=167.5\) loose m³/h. **Answer: C (168 Lm³/h).** The chart reading is approximate.

### 45. Increased unit-cost matrix
A 10% increase multiplies every purchase and delivery cost by 1.10:

\[
1.10\begin{bmatrix}6&4&2\\1&1&0.5\end{bmatrix}
=\begin{bmatrix}6.6&4.4&2.2\\1.1&1.1&0.55\end{bmatrix}.
\]

**Answer: D.**

### 46. Hydraulic-excavator production
Use the provided relation \(P=C\,S\,V\,B\,E\): 250 cycles/h, swing-depth factor 1.00, bucket capacity 0.57 m³, fill factor 0.95, and efficiency \(50/60\). Thus \(P=250(1.00)(0.57)(0.95)(50/60)=112.8\) Lm³/h. **Answer: A (113 Lm³/h).**

### 47. Toll-booth queue delay
Three booths each serve one vehicle every 6 s, for a combined capacity of 30 vehicles/min. Using each 10-minute arrival interval and the cumulative arrival/service curves, the queue at interval ends is 0, 100, 300, 250, 150, and 0 vehicles. The area under the queue curve is

\[
D=\frac{10}{2}[(0+100)+(100+300)+(300+250)+(250+150)+(150+0)]
=8000\,\mathrm{vehicle\!\cdot min}=133.3\,\mathrm{vehicle\!\cdot h}.
\]

**Answer: A (133 veh·h).**

### 48. Two-way traffic progression — key conflict
The hint gives \(C=2L/s\), where \(L\) is block length and \(s=50/3.6=13.89\) m/s. For the 140 m North–South block, \(C=20.16\) s, rounded to 20 s; speed is \((2L/C)(3.6)=50.4\) km/h. For the 240 m East–West block, \(C=34.56\) s, rounded to 35 s; speed is \((2L/C)(3.6)=49.4\) km/h. None of the listed pairs gives both values. **Printed key: C (50.4, 43.2 km/h); the East–West value conflicts with the stated cycle method.**

### 49. Concrete unit mixes
Wall volume is \(28.6(1.22)(0.305)=10.642\,\mathrm{m^3}\). Absolute non-air batch volume is

\[
V_b=\frac{136}{2.60(1000)}+\frac{95}{2.65(1000)}+\frac{42.5}{3.25(1000)}+\frac{22.5}{1000}=0.12373\,\mathrm{m^3}.
\]

With 5% air, yield per mix is \(0.12373/0.95=0.13025\,\mathrm{m^3}\), so \(10.642/0.13025=81.7\) mixes. **Answer: A (81.7 unit mixes).**

## Questions 50–69

### 50. Hazard
The stem describes circumstances or deviation from standard conditions that could permit an accident. “Hazard” is the broad term for such a potentially harmful circumstance; an unsafe act would be a person's behavior. **Answer: A (hazard), matching the key.**

### 51. Coastal-engineering environmental factor
The scan asks for the single most important environmental factor in coastal engineering. Of the choices, **wave** is the intended factor. **Answer: D.**

### 52. Concrete curing
Preventing moisture loss while maintaining a temperature that supports hydration and protects the concrete during early hardening describes curing. **Answer: A.**

### 53. Risk
Risk combines the likelihood of a hazardous event with the severity of its consequences. **Answer: C.**

### 54. Coastal-defense design condition
The governing severe case is the combined unusually high water level and severe wave action. **Answer: D.**

### 55. Gravity waves
Wind transfers energy to water; gravity is the restoring force that returns displaced particles toward equilibrium. Those are gravity waves. **Answer: D.**

### 56. Traffic-capacity parameter
Density is vehicles per unit roadway length, which is the quantity tied to cars occupying a given length of road. Volume (flow) is vehicles per unit time. **Answer: B (density).** The wording “more accurate parameter” is imprecise, but the stated reason identifies density.

### 57. Safe design for construction
The keyed set is I, III, IV, and V: the structure, construction equipment, on-site facilities, and construction manpower. The listed subsystems in II concern the completed building's services rather than the construction-safety elements intended by this question. **Answer: D, matching the key.**

### 58. Concrete shrinkage
The reduction in concrete volume as it hardens, especially in wetter mixes, is shrinkage; curing helps limit its effects. **Answer: D.**

### 59. Tack coat — key conflict
The single initial bituminous application to an existing bituminous, Portland-cement-concrete, or block surface is a tack coat (A). A prime coat is applied to an untreated granular base. **Correct term: A.** The printed key says B (prime coat), conflicting with the definition and choices.

### 60. Ideal road capacity
The conventional ideal capacity used in the item is 2,000 vehicles per hour. **Answer: B.**

### 61. PERT time estimates
PERT uses three time estimates, weights the most-likely estimate most heavily, and is based on a beta-distribution model for activity times. All three statements are true. **Answer: D (all of the above).**

### 62. Material-response categories
The broad categories listed are mechanical, thermal, electrical, and chemical properties. **Answer: B.**

### 63. Vertical-angle measurement — key conflict
For a reliable vertical/zenith-angle observation, level the instrument, use the vertical crosshair, sight the target, and observe in direct and reverse faces. All four practices are valid. **Best answer: C (all of the above).** The printed key gives B, omitting the valid target-sighting step.

### 64. PERT completion probability — key conflict
The usual calculation assumes independent activity times and approximates the critical-path total duration as normal; it uses the expected path time and the path variance. Thus statement I is supported, while statement II (beta distribution for the total path time) is false and III overstates the required information. **Best answer: A (I only).** The printed key gives D, which does not follow the standard method.

### 65. Reliability
Reliability is the probability that a mechanism functions satisfactorily for a specified occasion or time interval. **Answer: C.**

### 66. Soil-compaction objectives
Proper compaction minimizes settlement, increases bearing capacity and stability, and reduces water penetration (statements I–IV). Statement V is not a general compaction objective. **Answer: B.**

### 67. Rail thrust without superelevation
On a curve, the train's outward centrifugal action presses the flange against the inner face of the outer rail. **Answer: D, matching the key.**

### 68. Critical path
The critical path is the path with the greatest expected duration through the network. **Answer: A.**

### 69. Normal-curve statements
A symmetric histogram need not have one peak, and a symmetric bell-shaped curve need not be normal. A normal curve is bell-shaped and symmetric, so only statement III is true. **Answer: C.**
