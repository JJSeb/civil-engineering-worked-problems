## Questions 1–10

### Q1 — Stock allocation
Let \(A+B=100{,}000\). The annual return condition is \(0.15A+0.10B=13{,}000\). Substituting \(B=100{,}000-A\) gives \(A=60{,}000\), \(B=40{,}000\). **Answer: D. Key check: D.**

### Q2 — Rope cuts; wording ambiguity
The intended three lengths are \(x,x+2,x+4\) ft. Their sum gives \(3x+6=213\), hence lengths 69, 71, and 73 ft. The cut locations measured from one end are 69 and \(69+71=140\) ft. The choices list “69 and 73,” apparently the first and last piece lengths rather than the two cut positions. **Key: A (69 and 73); the wording “where should he make the cuts?” is imprecise.**

### Q3 — Ball dropped downward
Set ground height h = 0: \(-16t^2-20t+180=0\). The positive root is \(t=\frac{-20+\sqrt{11920}}{32}\approx2.80\) s. The displayed equation's negative signs correctly represent downward initial velocity and gravity. **Answer: B (2.8 s). Key check: B.**

### Q9 — Three-point mountain geometry
Let the known elevation at C be 1621 m. The angle at C from B to A is \(32^\circ30'\); the angle at B from C to A is \(8^\circ40'\). With \(BC=928\) m, the triangle's third angle is \(138^\circ50'\). By the sine rule, (AC=928\sin\(8^\circ40'\)/\sin\(32^\circ30'\)\approx263) m. The elevation difference is \(AC\tan32^\circ30'\approx168\) m, so A is about 1789 m if A is above C. The diagram/phrasing does not clearly establish vertical reference and sight-line orientation, and this does not match the choices. **Key check: B \(2847 m\); geometry as legible does not support the keyed value.**

### Q16 — Cooling model

### Q23 — Car spring compression

### Q24 — Spring constant from falling ball

### Q25 — Power against friction
