Start with the easy marks

Five-minute recall

  • Before calculating: sketch the body, show loads/reactions, mark dimensions, then write equilibrium.
  • Units: 1 N/mm² = 1 MPa; 1 kN·m = 1,000 N·m. Convert force and length before substituting.
  • Statics: a pin has two planar reactions; a roller has one normal reaction; a fixed support also resists moment.
  • Friction: static friction adjusts up to its maximum. Use Fs,max = μsN only at impending slip.
  • Trusses: at an unloaded joint with two non-collinear members, both are zero-force members.
  • Materials tests: cylinder compression → concrete strength; sieve analysis → gradation; tensile test → stress–strain properties; slump → fresh-concrete workability.
  • Beam patterns: point load gives a shear jump; a uniform load gives a sloped shear line and a curved moment diagram. A peak/minimum moment often occurs where V = 0.
  • Code questions: identify the code edition and design method, separate service loads from factored loads, then apply the matching resistance or safety factor. Don’t mix LRFD and ASD steps.

01 · Start here

Engineering Mechanics

Fast points: draw the free-body diagram first, choose a moment center that removes unknowns, and keep units consistent.

Equilibrium

A body at rest has zero net force and zero net moment.

\[\sum F_x=0,\qquad \sum F_y=0,\qquad \sum M_O=0\]

Use a separate equation for each independent direction. In 2D, there are at most three independent equilibrium equations.

Friction: easy trap

\[0\le F_s\le \mu_sN,\qquad F_{s,\max}=\mu_sN,\qquad F_k=\mu_kN\]

Static friction is not automatically at its maximum. It adjusts to match the needed resistance, up to \(\mu_sN\).

Motion shortcuts

For constant acceleration:

\[v=v_0+at,\qquad s=v_0t+\tfrac12at^2,\qquad v^2=v_0^2+2as\]

For projectile motion, treat horizontal and vertical motion separately. At the top, \(v_y=0\); horizontal velocity stays constant if air resistance is ignored.

Work and energy

\[U=Fs\cos\phi,\qquad T_1+U_{1\to2}=T_2,\qquad T=\tfrac12mv^2\]

Only the component of force along the displacement does work. Impulse changes momentum: \(\mathbf J=\int\mathbf F\,dt=m(\mathbf v_2-\mathbf v_1)\).

Worked 1 · reactionsEasy points

A centered point load on a simple span

Question. A simply supported beam carries \(P=12\,\text{kN}\) at midspan. Find the vertical reactions.

  1. Draw the FBD. Replace the pin and roller by reactions \(R_A\) and \(R_B\).
  2. Take moments about A. \(R_B L-P(L/2)=0\), so \(R_B=P/2=6\,\text{kN}\).
  3. Use vertical equilibrium. \(R_A+R_B-P=0\), hence \(R_A=6\,\text{kN}\).

Answer: \(R_A=R_B=6\,\text{kN}\), upward.

Worked 2 · frictionCommon concept check

Static friction adjusts to the applied force

Question. A \(20\,\text{kg}\) block rests on a level floor. Let \(g=9.81\,\text{m/s}^2\), \(\mu_s=0.40\), and apply a horizontal force of \(50\,\text{N}\). Does it move?

  1. Vertical equilibrium gives \(N=mg=20(9.81)=196.2\,\text{N}\).
  2. The maximum static friction is \(F_{s,\max}=\mu_sN=0.40(196.2)=78.5\,\text{N}\).
  3. Since \(50<78.5\), friction can balance the applied force: \(F_s=50\,\text{N}\), opposite the push.

Answer: It stays at rest. Do not set friction to \(78.5\,\text{N}\) unless impending slip is stated.

Worked 3 · trussesZero-force member

Spot zero-force members before calculating

At an unloaded joint with exactly two non-collinear members, both member forces are zero. This rule is a quick check; it does not apply if an external force or support reaction acts at that joint.

Here: \(F_1=F_2=0\).

Remember the sign and location

  • For a section cut, assume unknown member forces are in tension; a negative answer means compression.
  • In a moment equation, use perpendicular distance to the force's line of action.
  • Range formula \(R=v_0^2\sin(2\theta)/g\) assumes launch and landing elevations are equal and air resistance is neglected.
02 · Strength of Materials + Materials Testing

Strength of Materials

For most quick problems, identify the load, the resisting area or section property, then keep units consistent. Stress is force per area; strain is dimensionless.

Easy points to lock in

  • Stress: internal force intensity. Axial normal stress is (\sigma=P/A).
  • Strain: deformation per original length, (\varepsilon=\Delta L/L). No units.
  • Hooke's law: within the linear elastic range, (\sigma=E\varepsilon).
  • Modulus: (E) is the slope of the initial straight part of the stress–strain curve.
  • Stiffness vs strength: high (E) means less elastic strain for a given stress; it does not by itself mean higher failure strength.

Stress–strain picture

Schematic only. Exact curve shape and yield behavior depend on the material.

Core formulas

Axial stress and elastic deformation\[\sigma=\frac{P}{A},\qquad \varepsilon=\frac{\Delta L}{L}=\frac{\sigma}{E},\qquad \Delta L=\frac{PL}{AE}\]
Free thermal expansion\[\Delta L=\alpha L\Delta T\]
Solid circular shaft in torsion\[\tau=\frac{T\rho}{J},\qquad J=\frac{\pi d^4}{32},\qquad \theta=\frac{TL}{GJ}\]
Close-coiled helical spring\[\delta=\frac{8PD^3n}{Gd^4},\qquad \tau_{\max}=K_w\frac{8PD}{\pi d^3},\qquad K_w=\frac{4C-1}{4C-4}+\frac{0.615}{C},\quad C=\frac{D}{d}\]
Axial load plus bending\[\sigma_x=\frac{P}{A}\pm\frac{My}{I}\]
Plane-stress principal stresses\[\sigma_{1,2}=\frac{\sigma_x+\sigma_y}{2}\pm\sqrt{\left(\frac{\sigma_x-\sigma_y}{2}\right)^2+\tau_{xy}^2}\]
Maximum shear stress (Tresca)\[\tau_{\max}=\frac{\sigma_1-\sigma_3}{2}\]

Symbols: P axial force or spring load, A area, L original length, E Young's modulus, α thermal expansion coefficient, T torque, ρ radial position, J polar moment, G shear modulus. For a spring, D is mean coil diameter, d is wire diameter, and n is active coils. In combined stress, select the plus or minus sign from the point's location and loading direction.

Worked checks

1 · Axial stress and elongation easy

A steel rod has (P=50\,\text{kN}), (A=500\,\text{mm}^2), (L=2\,\text{m}), and (E=200\,\text{GPa}). Find the stress and elastic elongation.

  1. Use (1\,\text{MPa}=1\,\text{N/mm}^2): \[\sigma=\frac{50{,}000}{500}=100\,\text{N/mm}^2=100\,\text{MPa}.\]
  2. Convert (E=200\,\text{GPa}=200{,}000\,\text{N/mm}^2), (L=2{,}000\,\text{mm}): \[\Delta L=\frac{(50{,}000)(2{,}000)}{(500)(200{,}000)}=1.0\,\text{mm}.\]

Answer: (\sigma=100\,\text{MPa}); elongation (=1.0\,\text{mm}).

2 · Thermal expansion easy

A (12\,\text{m}) bar is free to expand. Take (\alpha=12\times10^{-6}/^\circ\text{C}) and temperature rise (\Delta T=30^\circ\text{C}). Find (\Delta L).

\[\Delta L=\alpha L\Delta T=(12\times10^{-6})(12)(30)=0.00432\,\text{m}=4.32\,\text{mm}.\]

Answer: (4.32\,\text{mm}) expansion. If restrained, this free-expansion result is not the final displacement; restraint creates thermal force/stress.

3 · Concrete cylinder compressive strength testing

A (150\,\text{mm})-diameter cylinder fails at (530\,\text{kN}). Find its compressive strength.

  1. Area: \[A=\frac{\pi d^2}{4}=\frac{\pi(150)^2}{4}=17{,}671\,\text{mm}^2.\]
  2. Convert (530\,\text{kN}=530{,}000\,\text{N}), then divide: \[f'_c=\frac{P_{\max}}{A}=\frac{530{,}000}{17{,}671}=30.0\,\text{N/mm}^2=30.0\,\text{MPa}.\]

Answer: (30.0\,\text{MPa}). Strength is maximum load divided by the loaded cross-sectional area.

Materials-testing recall: quick marks

Concrete compression test
Record maximum load; calculate (f'_c=P_{\max}/A). Keep diameter/area and force units consistent.
Tensile test
Load a specimen in tension; use the stress–strain response to identify elastic modulus, yield behavior, and tensile strength.
Aggregate grading
Sieve analysis gives particle-size distribution; percent retained and percent passing are cumulative ideas.
Soil index/compaction tests
Atterberg limits describe consistency of fine-grained soil. Proctor compaction relates dry unit weight to water content; know OMC and maximum dry unit weight.
Spring recall: more active coils or a larger mean coil diameter makes the spring more flexible; thicker wire makes it much stiffer because d⁴ is in the deflection denominator. Kw is the Wahl factor, combining curvature and direct-shear effects. If your course uses separate factors, follow its exact convention and do not apply both the full Wahl factor and an additional direct-shear factor.
Correction to the source note: a cantilever under uniform load has the maximum bending moment at the fixed support, (M_{\text{fixed}}=wL^2/2) in magnitude; the free end moment is zero. Also, for helical springs, (K_s) and Wahl factor notation vary—do not label the simple direct-shear correction factor as the Wahl factor unless the full formula matches your class convention.
Unit + sign check
(\text{N}/\text{mm}^2=\text{MPa}); convert kN to N and GPa to N/mm² before substituting. Tension is usually positive and compression negative under the usual sign convention; follow the problem's convention.

Theory of Structures

Read the structure, draw the free-body diagram, then use equilibrium. Check the sign and units before choosing an answer.

Easy points to lock in

  • Pin support: usually provides (A_x) and (A_y). Roller: one reaction normal to its surface. Fixed support: two reactions and a moment in a 2D frame.
  • Load resultants: a uniform load (w) over length (L) becomes (W=wL), acting at the segment midpoint.
  • Beam relations: (dM/dx=V); with downward load (w), (dV/dx=-w). An extremum of (M) often occurs where (V=0).
  • Sign convention: sagging moment is commonly positive and hogging negative. Follow the convention used by the question.
  • Truss count: for a plane pin-jointed truss, (i=m+r-2j). A zero count is necessary for a determinate truss, but geometry must also be stable.
  • Moment distribution: (DF_i=k_i/sum k); for a prismatic member with far end fixed, (k=4EI/L), and carry-over factor (=1/2).

Simply supported beam under full-span UDL

For a beam of span (L) with uniform load (w):

\[R_A=R_B=\frac{wL}{2},\qquad V(x)=\frac{wL}{2}-wx,\qquad M(x)=\frac{wL}{2}x-\frac{wx^2}{2}.\]

WORKED · UDL BEAM

Find the reactions and maximum moment

A simply supported (6\,\text{m}) beam carries (4\,\text{kN/m}) over the full span. Find (R_A), (R_B), and the maximum moment.

  1. Replace the UDL: (W=wL=4(6)=24\,\text{kN}), acting at (3\,\text{m}) from A.
  2. Use symmetry or moments: (R_A=R_B=24/2=12\,\text{kN}).
  3. Locate the peak: (V(x)=12-4x=0\Rightarrow x=3\,\text{m}).
  4. Evaluate moment: (M(3)=12(3)-4(3^2)/2=\boxed{18\,\text{kN}\!\cdot\!\text{m}}).

Quick check: reactions add to (24\,\text{kN}), the total applied load.

WORKED · TRUSS COUNT

Is the simple triangular truss determinate?

A triangular plane truss has (j=3) joints, (m=3) members, and a pin plus roller ((r=3) reaction components).

  1. Apply the plane-truss count: (i=m+r-2j).
  2. Substitute: (i=3+3-2(3)=\boxed{0}). The count says statically determinate.
  3. Check the shape too: a non-collinear triangle is geometrically stable. A zero count alone would not prove stability for every layout.

Influence lines: the easy distinction

An influence line tracks the value of one response as a unit load moves. For reactions on a simple span, the lines are straight. For moment at a section (C), (AC=a), (CB=b), and (L=a+b):

\[y_M(x)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{xb}{L},&0\le x\le a,\\[4pt]\dfrac{a(L-x)}{L},&a\le x\le L.\end{cases}\]

The moment ordinate is zero at both supports and peaks at (C), with ordinate (ab/L).

Do not confuse shear and reaction influence lines. Shear at a section has a jump at the section; its exact signs depend on the adopted shear convention. The simple 0-to-1 triangle is the reaction influence line, not a general shear influence line.

Two formula checks from the source outline

  • For a cantilever with full-span UDL, the fixed-support moment magnitude is (wL^2/2); the free-end moment is zero. The free-end deflection magnitude is (wL^4/(8EI)).
  • For a simply supported beam, “ordinate 1 at the support and 0 at the opposite support” describes a support-reaction influence line, not the shear line at an arbitrary section.

Quick formulas from the source outline

Cantilever · full-span uniform load

For downward UDL (w) over cantilever length (L), with sagging positive:

\[M_{\text{fixed}}=-\frac{wL^2}{2},\qquad M_{\text{free}}=0,\qquad \delta_{\text{free}}=\frac{wL^4}{8EI}\;\text{(downward)}.\]

The fixed-end reaction moment has magnitude (wL^2/2); the internal section-moment sign depends on the chosen convention.

Moment distribution · stiffness and carry-over
\[DF_i=\frac{k_i}{\sum_j k_j},\qquad k=\begin{cases}\dfrac{4EI}{L},&\text{far end fixed},\\[3pt]\dfrac{3EI}{L},&\text{far end hinged.}\end{cases}\]

For a prismatic member with the far end fixed, the carry-over factor is (1/2) (same sign). With a hinged far end, carry-over is zero. These stiffnesses assume the near joint rotates while the far-end condition is as stated.

Three-moment equation · two adjacent spans

For spans (AB=L_1) and (BC=L_2), constant (EI) on both spans, no support settlement, and sagging-positive end moments:

\[M_A L_1+2M_B(L_1+L_2)+M_C L_2=-6\left(\frac{A_1\bar{x}_1}{L_1}+\frac{A_2\bar{x}_2}{L_2}\right).\]

(A_1,A_2) are areas of the simply supported bending-moment diagrams caused by the span loads considered separately. (\bar{x}_1) is measured from (A); (\bar{x}_2) is measured from (C) (the outer supports). Use signed areas if the chosen moment convention requires them.

The centroid-distance factors are required: an area divided by span length alone is dimensionally incomplete. If (EI) differs between spans or supports settle, use the generalized form with each span's (EI) and settlement terms; do not apply this simplified equation unchanged.
